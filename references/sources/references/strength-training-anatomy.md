# Strength Training Anatomy (Third Edition)

> [!tldr] *Strength Training Anatomy* (Frédéric Delavier) is a foundational anatomical reference detailing exercise mechanics, target muscle activation, joint leverage, structural variations, and injury prevention.

## Overview

Authored and illustrated by [[entities/frederic-delavier]], this work presents comprehensive anatomical breakdowns of over 400 exercises and variations, organized by major muscle compartments.

## Anatomical Sections & Page Map

1. **Arms** (pp. 8–31): Biceps brachii, brachialis, triceps brachii, forearm flexors/extensors.
2. **Shoulders** (pp. 32–60): Anterior, lateral, and posterior deltoids, rotator cuff musculature (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, subscapularis, teres minor).
3. **Chest** (pp. 61–84): Pectoralis major (sternal and clavicular heads), pectoralis minor, serratus anterior.
4. **Back** (pp. 85–121): Latissimus dorsi, trapezius (upper, middle, lower), rhomboids, teres major, erector spinae.
5. **Legs** (pp. 122–154): Quadriceps femoris, hamstrings, adductors, gastrocnemius, soleus.
6. **Buttocks** (pp. 155–169): Gluteus maximus, gluteus medius, gluteus minimus.
7. **Abdomen** (pp. 170–196): Rectus abdominis, external and internal obliques, transverse abdominis.

## Key Core Insights

- **Morphological Variants**: Individual variations in bone length, muscle tendon lengths, and origin/insertion points alter joint leverage, changing exercise effectiveness and injury vulnerability (Delavier, pp. 4–6).
- **Regional Targeting**: Adjusting hand grip width, stance width, or angle of resistance shifts load between individual muscle heads (e.g., incline press emphasizing clavicular pectoralis, narrow grip chin-ups emphasizing lower lats) (Delavier, pp. 34, 62, 86).
- **Joint Safety**: Over-extending or forcing unnatural movement paths under heavy load increases risks of shoulder subacromial impingement, biceps tendon rupture, or lumbar disc herniation (Delavier, pp. 38, 70, 126).
