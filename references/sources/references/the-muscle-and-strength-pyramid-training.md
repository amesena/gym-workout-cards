# The Muscle and Strength Pyramid: Training (Second Edition)

> [!tldr] *The Muscle and Strength Pyramid: Training* (Eric Helms, Andy Morgan, Andrea Valdez) establishes a structured 6-level hierarchy prioritizing resistance training variables from most critical to fine-tuning details.

## Overview

Written by [[entities/eric-helms]] alongside Andy Morgan and Andrea Valdez, this text bridges academic exercise science and practical gym application. It organizes training design into a logical pyramid framework to prevent trainees from focusing on minor details before establishing core priorities.

## The 6-Level Hierarchy of Training Priorities

1. **Level 1: [[concepts/adherence]]** (pp. 31–40): Program sustainability, realistic scheduling, enjoyment, and consistency.
2. **Level 2: Volume, Intensity, Frequency** (pp. 41–80): Weekly set volume (10–20 sets/muscle), loading intensity, RIR targets, and volume distribution.
3. **Level 3: [[concepts/progressive-overload-models|Progression]]** (pp. 81–122): Managing progressive overload across Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced stages using linear, wave, and block periodization.
4. **Level 4: [[concepts/exercise-selection]]** (pp. 123–140): Compound vs isolation efficiency, weak point training, movement patterns, and autoregulated substitutions.
5. **Level 5: [[concepts/rest-intervals|Rest Periods]]** (pp. 141–150): Inter-set rest durations (2–5 min for main movements vs 1–2 min for accessories) to preserve performance.
6. **Level 6: [[concepts/repetition-tempo|Tempo]]** (pp. 151–160): Repetition velocity, natural eccentric speeds, and why arbitrary time-under-tension protocols are secondary.

## Key Core Takeaways

- **Adherence Over Perfection**: The best training program on paper is useless if a lifter cannot adhere to it consistently due to lifestyle conflict or unrealistic volume (Helms et al., p. 32).
- **RIR-Based Autoregulation**: Using Reps in Reserve (RIR) to quantify proximity to failure accounts for daily fluctuations in fatigue, stress, and recovery (Helms et al., pp. 45–50; [[concepts/rpe-and-rir-autoregulation]]).
- **Volume Landmarks**: 10 to 20 sets per muscle group per week represents the primary evidence-based volume target for intermediate and advanced lifters (Helms et al., pp. 52–55).
