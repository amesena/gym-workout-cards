# Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning (Fourth Edition)

> [!tldr] *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning* (G. Gregory Haff & N. Travis Triplett, NSCA) is the definitive textbook establishing evidence-based standards for strength science, human performance, exercise physiology, and athletic program design.

## Overview

Published by the National Strength and Conditioning Association ([[entities/nsca]]), this 26-chapter text serves as the scientific foundation for Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist (CSCS) credentialing and elite athletic performance design.

## Core Chapters & Domain Breakdown

1. **Structure & Function of Body Systems** (Ch. 1, pp. 38–103): Musculoskeletal, neuromuscular, cardiovascular, and respiratory physiology.
2. **Biomechanics of Resistance Exercise** (Ch. 2, pp. 104–173): Skeletal levers (1st, 2nd, 3rd class), mechanical advantage, force-velocity curve, torque, and joint safety.
3. **[[concepts/bioenergetics|Bioenergetics of Exercise]]** (Ch. 3, pp. 174–226): ATP-PCr phosphagen system, anaerobic glycolysis, oxidative system, substrate depletion, and metabolic specificity.
4. **[[concepts/endocrine-responses-to-resistance-training|Endocrine Responses]]** (Ch. 4, pp. 227–280): Anabolic hormone cascades (Testosterone, GH, IGF-1) vs catabolic signaling (Cortisol) and acute training triggers.
5. **Anaerobic & Aerobic Adaptations** (Ch. 5–6, pp. 281–383): Neuromuscular recruitment, rate coding, doublet firing, bone mineral density, and connective tissue remodeling.
6. **Testing, Technique & Program Design** (Ch. 13–18, pp. 729–1259): Needs analysis, loading parameters, exercise ordering, and rest interval structures.
7. **[[concepts/stretch-shortening-cycle|Plyometrics & SSC]]** (Ch. 19, pp. 1260–1397): Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC), amortization phase, muscle spindles, and GTO autogenic inhibition.
8. **[[concepts/periodization-for-hypertrophy|Periodization]]** (Ch. 22, pp. 1579–1629): Matveyev's classical model, macrocycle/mesocycle/microcycle partitioning, and transition phases.
9. **[[concepts/overtraining-syndrome|Overtraining & Recovery]]** (Ch. 24, pp. 1679–1726): Functional Overreaching (FOR), Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR), Overtraining Syndrome (OTS), and sympathetic vs parasympathetic markers.
