# Proximity to Failure

Proximity to failure measures the degree of effort exerted during a set, quantified by how many additional repetitions could have been completed before momentary muscular failure (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 87; Helms et al., pp. 45–50).

## Henneman's Size Principle & Motor Unit Recruitment

According to Henneman's size principle, motor units are recruited in an orderly fashion from smallest/low-threshold (Type I) to largest/high-threshold (Type II) (Schoenfeld, Ch. 2, p. 37).
- With **heavy loads (>80% 1RM)**, high-threshold motor units are recruited immediately.
- With **light-to-moderate loads**, high-threshold motor units are only recruited in the final reps as lower-threshold fibers fatigue near failure (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 87; Helms et al., p. 46).

## Quantification via RPE & RIR

- **Reps in Reserve (RIR)**: Estimated reps remaining before failure (0 RIR = failure, 1 RIR = 1 rep left) (Helms et al., pp. 45–48).
- **RPE (RIR Scale)**: RPE 10 = 0 RIR, RPE 9 = 1 RIR, RPE 8 = 2 RIR, RPE 7 = 3 RIR (Helms et al., p. 48; [[concepts/rpe-and-rir-autoregulation]]).

## Failure vs Near-Failure (1–3 RIR Target)

Meta-analytic evidence and coaching data confirm that training to **1–3 RIR** produces comparable muscle hypertrophy to complete failure (0 RIR), while generating significantly less central fatigue and permitting higher total [[concepts/training-volume]] (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, pp. 88–90; Helms et al., pp. 49–51).
- **RIR Stop Rules**: Terminating sets at 1–2 RIR on multi-joint compound movements prevents excessive fatigue, reserving 0 RIR for single-joint isolation lifts or final mesocycle weeks (Helms et al., p. 50).
