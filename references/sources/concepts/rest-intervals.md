# Rest Intervals

Rest intervals denote the recovery period taken between sets of resistance exercise (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 110).

## Classification & Effects

1. **Short Rest (<60 seconds)**: Increases acute [[concepts/metabolic-stress]] and growth hormone secretion, but significantly reduces repetition capacity on subsequent sets due to incomplete phosphocreatine (PCr) resynthesis and peripheral fatigue (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 110).
2. **Moderate Rest (1–2 minutes)**: Balanced compromise suitable for single-joint assistance exercises.
3. **Long Rest (2–3+ minutes)**: Allows full recovery of ATP-PCr stores and central drive, maximizing load lifted and reps completed on subsequent sets (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 111).

## Impact on Hypertrophy

Longer rest periods (2–3 minutes) have been demonstrated by Schoenfeld et al. to elicit **greater muscle hypertrophy** than short rest periods (1 minute) in multi-joint compound movements, primarily because longer rest preserves total [[concepts/training-volume]] load across sets (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, pp. 111–113).

## Practical Recommendation

- Rest **2–3+ minutes** on multi-joint exercises (squats, bench presses, deadlifts).
- Rest **1–2 minutes** on single-joint isolation exercises.
