# Progressive Overload Models

Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed upon the body during resistance training (Helms et al., pp. 81–82). Helms categorizes progression models according to **training age**:

## 1. Novice Progression (Session-to-Session / Weekly)

- **Mechanism**: Novices adapt rapidly, recovering between sessions (Helms et al., p. 83).
- **Model**: Single Linear Progression. Add load (e.g., +1–2.5 kg) to the bar every session or week while maintaining target rep ranges (Helms et al., pp. 83–85).

## 2. Intermediate Progression (Wave / Microcycle Periodization)

- **Mechanism**: Adaptations slow; weekly periodization is required to manage fatigue (Helms et al., p. 88).
- **Models**:
  - **Linear Wave Progression**: Increasing load while decreasing reps across 3–4 week cycles (e.g., Week 1: 3x10 @ 70%, Week 2: 3x8 @ 75%, Week 3: 3x6 @ 80%, Week 4: Deload) (Helms et al., pp. 89–92).
  - **Double Progression**: Fix load and increase reps across sessions until upper limit is reached (e.g., 3x8-12; when 3x12 is achieved, increase load and reset to 3x8) (Helms et al., pp. 93–95).

## 3. Advanced Progression (Block & Autoregulated Periodization)

- **Mechanism**: Gains require multi-week mesocycles organized into distinct accumulation, intensification, and peak phases (Helms et al., pp. 100–105).
- **Model**: Autoregulated Block Periodization utilizing RIR targets and velocity loss to manage overreaching and schedule deloads (Helms et al., pp. 106–112; [[concepts/rpe-and-rir-autoregulation]]).
