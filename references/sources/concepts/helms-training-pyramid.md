# Helms' Training Pyramid

Helms' Training Pyramid is a conceptual model designed to prioritize variables in strength and hypertrophy program design (Helms et al., pp. 28–30).

```
          /         /  \  Level 6: Tempo / Rep Speed
        /----       /      \  Level 5: Rest Periods
      /--------     /          \  Level 4: Exercise Selection
    /------------   /              \  Level 3: Progression
  /---------------- /                  \  Level 2: Volume, Intensity, Frequency
/--------------------/                      \  Level 1: ADHERENCE
/------------------------```

## Levels Breakdown

1. **Level 1: [[concepts/adherence|Adherence]]**: The foundation. A program must be realistic, enjoyable, and sustainable to yield long-term results (Helms et al., pp. 31–40).
2. **Level 2: Volume, Intensity, Frequency**: The primary quantitative drivers of adaptation. Target ~10–20 weekly sets per muscle group, appropriate loading (% 1RM & RIR), and frequency (Helms et al., pp. 41–80; [[concepts/training-volume]]).
3. **Level 3: [[concepts/progressive-overload-models|Progression]]**: Systematically advancing performance over time through linear, wave, or autoregulated periodization models tailored to training age (Helms et al., pp. 81–122).
4. **Level 4: [[concepts/exercise-selection|Exercise Selection]]**: Choosing movements based on biomechanics, muscle architecture, joint safety, and individual movement efficiency (Helms et al., pp. 123–140).
5. **Level 5: [[concepts/rest-intervals|Rest Periods]]**: Recovery between sets (2–5 min for heavy compound lifts, 1–2 min for isolation) to maintain performance and volume load (Helms et al., pp. 141–150).
6. **Level 6: [[concepts/repetition-tempo|Tempo]]**: Repetition speed and cadence. Secondary to overall volume load; maintain a natural concentric pace and controlled eccentric (Helms et al., pp. 151–160).

## Core Principle

Addressing higher-level variables (e.g., tweaking tempo or rest intervals) before securing foundation levels (Adherence and Volume/Intensity/Frequency) results in suboptimal long-term adaptation (Helms et al., p. 29).
