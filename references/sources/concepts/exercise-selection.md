# Exercise Selection

Exercise selection involves choosing specific movements and variations to maximize mechanical stimulus and safely target muscle compartments (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 99; Delavier, pp. 4–7).

## Multi-Joint vs Single-Joint Exercises

- **Multi-Joint (Compound) Exercises**: Involve multiple joints and dynamic muscle groups (e.g., squat, bench press, chin-up). Efficient for loading large muscle masses and generating systemic mechanical tension (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 100).
- **Single-Joint (Isolation) Exercises**: Target individual muscles across a single joint (e.g., leg extension, bicep curl, lateral raise). Crucial for fully activating bi-articular muscles or muscle heads poorly recruited during multi-joint movements (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 101; Delavier, pp. 8, 34).

## Anatomical Biomechanics & Regional Recruitment

Muscles are compartmentalized into distinct anatomical regions with varying pennation angles, leverage, and neuromuscular innervation (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 102).
- **[[concepts/regional-muscle-recruitment|Regional Targeting]]**: Adjusting grip width, foot placement, body inclination, or angle of resistance shifts mechanical torque between specific muscle heads (e.g., incline press targeting clavicular pectoralis, overhead extensions stretching long head triceps) (Delavier, pp. 22, 64; [[concepts/biomechanics-and-kinesiology]]).
- **Individual Morphological Safety**: Matching exercise selection to individual bone leverage and joint structure (e.g., shoulder acromion type, femur-to-torso ratio) minimizes joint impingement and tissue trauma (Delavier, pp. 4–6, 38).

## Exercise Variation Guidelines

While variation prevents accommodation and ensures complete partitioning, excessively frequent variation hinders progressive overload due to persistent motor re-learning (Schoenfeld, Ch. 4, p. 104).
