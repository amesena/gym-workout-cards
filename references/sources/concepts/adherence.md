# Adherence

Adherence occupies Level 1 (the base) of [[concepts/helms-training-pyramid]], establishing that no training program can produce results if the lifter cannot execute it consistently over time (Helms et al., p. 31).

## Key Components of Adherence

1. **Realistic Scheduling**: A training schedule must fit a lifter's work, family, and lifestyle commitments. Programming 6 days/week for a busy individual often leads to missed sessions, guilt, and abandonment (Helms et al., pp. 32–33).
2. **Enjoyment & Preference**: Lifter preference directly impacts effort and psychological engagement. Incorporating exercises or splits a lifter enjoys enhances adherence and long-term compliance (Helms et al., p. 34).
3. **Flexibility & Autoregulation**: Rigid programs that do not accommodate life stressors or fatigue encourage burnout. Incorporating flexible scheduling or autoregulated sessions preserves long-term momentum (Helms et al., p. 35; [[concepts/rpe-and-rir-autoregulation]]).

## Practical Implementation

- Match weekly frequency (e.g., 3, 4, or 5 days/week) to what is consistently repeatable year-round, rather than short-term peak periods.
- Distribute [[concepts/training-volume]] into sessions that fit realistic time budgets (e.g., 45–60 min per session).
